 Waves Over Weights

 
 A full reading of Miller, Brincat & Roy, “Analog Cognition and Consciousness,”
 J. Neurosci. 46(33) e0711262026 — all 22 pages of argument and 200+ references —
 mapped against the machine-learning and hardware literature it never cites,
 with ten research directions that fall in the gap between the two fields.
 

 

 
 neuroscience strand
 machine-learning strand
 their sum — “the interaction is the computation” (Fig. 6 of the paper)
 

 
 Miller, Brincat and Roy argue that the brain is a hybrid computer: synaptic circuits store
 knowledge digitally, while traveling electric-field waves select, route, and compute
 over that knowledge in analog — and that consciousness is what a globally wave-organized
 cortex feels like from inside. This review takes the paper apart claim by claim, rebuilds the
 literature around it, and finds something the paper itself doesn't say: machine learning has
 spent the last three years independently reinventing half of this theory — the wrong half —
 while neither field cites the other.

 00 · Verdict
 Can this be mapped onto making neural networks better?

 

 
 Yes — and more literally than the authors realize

 
 The paper is a synthesis manifesto: it welds twenty-five years of the Miller lab's work —
 mixed selectivity, working-memory bursts, predictive routing, traveling waves, cytoelectric
 coupling, anesthesia dynamics — into one architecture. Synapses and spikes are the
 storage and content layer; mesoscale electric-field waves are the control and computation
 layer. No new data appears here; the new moves are conceptual: traveling waves as
 operators that shift population activity between coding subspaces, wave interference as
 literal analog computation, and consciousness as the globally integrated regime of that
 wave system.
 

 
 The mapping to AI is unusually concrete because deep learning already made the
 paper's foundational split without naming it: pretrained weights store; in-context
 activations select. What ML lacks is the paper's control layer — and here is the review's
 central finding: ML has imported the brain's waves as content (memory in
 wave-RNNs and oscillatory state-space models, binding in Kuramoto networks — four ICLR
 oral-level papers since 2023) but not as control (spatial gating stencils,
 wave-composed task programs, phase-multiplexed routing, a field medium). The control half is
 the half Miller's group argues actually makes cognition. Meanwhile the paper cites five
 computing works out of two hundred references and none of the ML strand. Two fields are
 converging on one architecture, blind to each other — and the unclaimed middle is mapped in
 §04 as ten concrete proposals, from an ephaptic side-channel for transformers to an
 anesthesiology-derived coherence metric for agents.
 

 

 
 01 · The paper, decomposed
 Eight claims, separately graded

 

 
 The paper's rhetoric moves fast between settled findings and fresh speculation; mapping it
 onto AI responsibly requires pulling those apart. Grades reflect the state of evidence as the
 paper itself presents it, cross-checked against the cited record: ●●●●
 established down to ●○○○ speculative.
 

 
 C1
 
 Cortical neurons are multifunctional, not specialized

 ●●●● established · 20+ years, many labs
 
 Mixed selectivity — single neurons whose tuning nonlinearly combines objects, order,
 rules, rewards — is the norm from prefrontal to primary sensory cortex (Rigotti 2013;
 Tye 2024). It expands representational capacity but creates the problem the whole theory
 answers: who routes signals through neurons that mean different things in different
 contexts? Synaptic weight changes are too slow, and a controller that addressed
 individual synapses would need implausible knowledge of the wiring.
 

 

 

 
 C2
 
 Alpha/beta carries control; gamma carries content; they push–pull

 ●●●○ strong · replicated across species and areas
 
 Slower rhythms (deep layers, feedback paths) suppress and sculpt faster gamma and spiking
 (superficial layers, feedforward paths). The spectrolaminar motif holds across mouse,
 marmoset, macaque, human, and a dozen-plus regions (Mendoza-Halliday 2024). Theta acts as
 a master clock that drags coupled beta/gamma across cortex. This is the paper's
 best-evidenced structural fact.
 

 

 

 
 C3
 
 Spatial computing: control signals are inhibitory “stencils” over the cortical sheet

 ●●○○ emerging · confirmed chiefly by the authors' own lab
 
 Context and goals appear as spatial patterns of alpha/beta that mask where gamma/spiking
 may express — so the same input lands in different subpopulations under different task
 contexts, with no synaptic rewiring. The same stencil works for arbitrary content, and the
 same population serves arbitrary tasks: compositional reuse in both directions. Chen et
 al. 2026 (Curr. Biol.) confirmed key predictions in macaque PFC — including that
 stencil–context mismatch predicts behavioral errors — and Fang et al. 2025, from
 an independent lab, found oscillatory control of working-memory geometry. Mixed
 selectivity (C1) then falls out as an explanation, not an axiom: a broadly tuned
 neuron inside the stencil in one context and outside it in another looks
 context-selective.
 

 

 

 
 C4
 
 Ephaptic coupling gives fields a direct, fast, non-synaptic handle on spiking

 ●●○○ contested at cognitive scale · solid at cellular scale
 
 Endogenous fields demonstrably bias spike timing where tissue is dense and neurons sit
 near threshold (Anastassiou 2011; Han 2018; 2025 bioprinted-circuit validations). Fields
 are continuous where spiking is sparse, reach millimeters where spikes are local, and
 propagate effectively instantaneously. The paper adds two frontier extensions —
 astrocytes (1 : 1.4 to neurons in humans) as non-spiking field contributors, and fields
 tuning cytoskeletal infrastructure — that are real observations carrying heavy
 speculative load. Whether ephaptic effects are a load-bearing computational channel
 rather than a weak bias remains the theory's most attackable joint.
 

 

 

 
 C5
 
 Traveling waves are the state-transition operators — “spatial computing in motion”

 ●○○○ hypothesis · new in this paper, correlational support
 
 Waves sweep excitability across cortex like radar (phase predicts detection at the
 swept location); they appear precisely when population codes reorganize — stimulus onset,
 loading into working memory, movement initiation. The paper's genuinely novel proposal:
 an alpha/beta traveling wave is the operator that moves population activity from
 one orthogonal coding subspace to another (sensory → memory), and low-dimensional
 “rotational dynamics” seen in population recordings are the spike-level shadow of waves
 pulling activity along. Sleep's rotating waves organizing spike-timing-dependent
 plasticity (Muller 2016) extends the same idea to consolidation.
 

 

 

 
 C6
 
 Wave interference performs analog computation

 ●○○○ hypothesis · physically plausible, no direct neural demonstration
 
 Where waves superpose they add and subtract — massively parallel, energy-cheap
 computation in the medium itself. Two context-waves summing compute an intersection of
 constraints (their Fig. 7: order × report-mode = four activation landscapes from two
 signals). Waves act as “dynamic spatiotemporal basis functions” able to synthesize any
 control landscape. Boldest concrete example: contour-evoked waves in visual cortex
 interfering to compute the grassfire/medial-axis transform of a shape (Blum 1973). The
 supporting citations are from physics and engineering — wave physics as a trainable RNN
 (Hughes 2019), metamaterial computers, analog AI chips — because the neural demonstration
 doesn't exist yet.
 

 

 

 
 C7
 
 Anesthetics converge on wave disorganization, not circuit shutdown

 ●●●○ strong · convergent multi-drug evidence
 
 Propofol (GABA), ketamine (NMDA), and dexmedetomidine (α2) act on unrelated receptors yet
 converge on the same systems-level outcome: high-power, temporally misaligned slow-delta
 waves and a fragmented cortex, with synaptic circuitry left largely intact (Bardon 2025;
 Eisen 2026). Thalamic stimulation partially restores both waves and responsiveness. The
 inference: consciousness tracks the integrity of large-scale wave organization, not any
 particular neurochemistry. Methodologically this is the paper's most elegant argument —
 many mechanistically different perturbations converging on one dynamical signature is
 evidence the signature is load-bearing.
 

 

 

 
 C8
 
 Consciousness is the globally integrated wave-organized state

 ●○○○ speculative · inherits every open problem of consciousness science
 
 Positioned against the field: like Global Workspace, it wants flexible global
 integration, but via field dynamics rather than spike broadcast; like IIT, it prizes
 integrated self-influence, but locates it in wave dynamics rather than a posterior “hot
 zone”; like higher-order and predictive-coding theories, top-down models organize
 lower-level activity — here the very act of wave-borne top-down organization is
 the conscious integration, with no separate observer. Notably absent from the reference
 list: McFadden's CEMI and other electromagnetic-field theories of consciousness — the
 closest prior claims that “the field is the thing.” The omission reads as deliberate
 positioning: mechanism-first field computation, carefully fenced off from
 field-consciousness metaphysics.
 

 

 

 
 02 · The two literatures
 One architecture, converged on twice

 

 
 Read as a literature review, the paper sits at the junction of five strands. Two of them —
 the controversy ledger and the computing mirror — are where its blind spots, and therefore
 the opportunities, live.
 

 
 A The scaffolding: the lab's own quarter-century neuro

 
 Nearly every mechanism has a Miller-lab origin paper: mixed selectivity (Warden 2007 →
 Rigotti 2013), gamma/beta working-memory bursts (Lundqvist 2016), predictive routing
 (Bastos 2020), interhemispheric handshakes (Brincat 2021), traveling waves in PFC working
 memory (Bhattacharya 2022), spatial computing (Lundqvist 2023), cytoelectric coupling
 (Pinotsis 2023), anesthesia dynamics with Emery Brown's group (Bastos 2021 → Bardon 2025 →
 Eisen 2026), and the empirical capstone, Chen 2026. This is a strength (coherence) and a
 weakness (evidence concentration — see strand E).
 

 

 
 B The allies: rhythms-as-computation neuroscience neuro

 
 The theory quilts together Buzsáki's Rhythms of the Brain, Fries's
 communication-through-coherence, Lisman & Jensen's theta–gamma code, Singer's
 binding-by-synchrony lineage, Ermentrout & Kleinfeld's wave mathematics, the
 Muller–Sejnowski–Reynolds traveling-wave program, Jacobs's human intracranial waves, and
 Fiebelkorn & Kastner's rhythmic attention. What the paper adds to this coalition is a
 job description: the rhythms aren't just coordination or clocking — they are the control
 plane and, at intersections, the ALU.
 

 

 
 C The consciousness landscape neuro

 
 GNWT, IIT, higher-order theories, Graziano's attention schema, the nested-observer model,
 Hunt & Schooler's resonance theory — the paper triangulates among all of them (§C8).
 Context the paper omits: the 2025 COGITATE adversarial collaboration left both GNWT and IIT
 wounded, which is precisely the vacuum a mechanism-first wave theory is stepping into. Its
 anesthesia evidence is its entry ticket; its liability is that “organized global
 integration” is currently a description, not a measurement — a gap proposal P8 turns into
 an instrument.
 

 

 
 D The computing mirror — what the paper doesn't know it's describing ml / hardware

 
 Out of 200+ references, five touch computing (an analog-computation survey, wave physics
 as a trainable RNN, two analog-hardware chips, a metamaterial computer). Missing is the
 entire convergent ML strand of the last three years:
 

 
 Oscillator units: AKOrN — Kuramoto neurons whose phase-synchrony
 binds features — improved object discovery, adversarial robustness, calibration, and
 Sudoku-style reasoning (ICLR 2025 oral); complex-valued synchrony nets and rotating
 features before it.

 Waves as memory: Keller et al.'s wave-RNNs — traveling waves in the
 hidden state act as a registry of the recent past, matching gated RNNs with fewer
 parameters; waves integrating spatial information through time (2025).

 Oscillatory state-space models: LinOSS — forced harmonic oscillators
 as the recurrence, beating Mamba by ~2× on 50k-length sequences (ICLR 2025 oral);
 damped D-LinOSS after it.

 Synchrony as attention: Selective Synchronization Attention (Feb
 2026) — attention weights are Kuramoto phase-locking strengths; positional
 encoding absorbed into oscillator frequencies. The first true bridge paper, and it cites
 the physics, not the neuroscience.

 Emergent interference algorithms: mechanistic interpretability's
 grokking results — transformers forced to learn modular arithmetic spontaneously invent
 Fourier features and trig-identity circuits. Gradient descent discovers phase-interference
 computation on its own when the task demands it; nobody has promoted it to a primitive.

 Hardware: photonic processors compute matrix products by wave
 interference at 100+ TOPS/W; analog in-memory chips; oscillator/Ising machines —
 and a deep historical root, Goto's 1950s parametron computers, which ran logic in
 oscillation phase.

 
 
 A discoverability accident deepens the mutual blindness: every natural search term is
 squatted. “Neural fields” means coordinate-implicit representations in ML; “analog
 computing” means in-memory matmul accelerators; “wave networks” is scattered across audio
 models. The two literatures could not find each other by keyword if they tried.
 

 

 
 E The controversy ledger — what the paper underplays neuro

 
 Causality vs. bookkeeping. The oldest objection: oscillations as
 exhaust, not engine. The paper cites the debate (Singer & Effenberger 2025) but
 resolves it by assertion; the ephaptic channel (C4) is its causal escape hatch, and that
 channel's cognitive-scale strength is exactly what's contested (Anastassiou & Koch's
 own review title: “why bother?”).

 Waves from delays. Davis 2021 showed traveling waves emerge naturally
 from axonal conduction delays — cited approvingly, but it cuts both ways: waves may be
 what recurrent tissue does, not what it's for.

 Own-lab concentration. C3 and C5's direct evidence is
 Miller-lab macaque data; Fang 2025 is the main independent support. The stencil readout
 also depends on spatial LFP patterns, where volume conduction makes localization claims
 delicate.

 The consciousness chapter is the thinnest. Anesthetic convergence
 shows organized waves are necessary-ish; nothing shown makes them
 sufficient, and GNWT/IIT can absorb the same data.

 Two strands developing the same architecture in parallel. The neuroscience strand worked out
 waves as control (stencils, routing, state transitions); the ML strand independently
 built waves as content (memory, binding) and stopped there. The proposals in §04
 live in the convergence neither strand has entered.
 

 

 
 The asymmetry deserves emphasis because it defines where not-obvious work lives. When ML
 borrowed traveling waves, it borrowed the sensory-cortex literature (Muller,
 Sejnowski, Reynolds) — waves that carry and integrate stimulus content. The
 executive reading of waves — Miller's stencils gating a multifunctional substrate,
 contexts composed by wave summation, a theta clock scheduling it all — has no ML translation
 at all. Everything in §04 grows from that missing half.

 04 · The connective leaps
 Ten proposals from the unclaimed middle

 

 
 Each proposal names the two strands it fuses, why the spot is verifiably empty (checked
 against the August 2026 literature), the cheapest first experiment, and the honest risk.
 They are ordered within groups by tractability — several are a single-GPU project.
 

 Architecture

 
 P1The Ephaptic Channel

 field coupling · astrocytes (C4) ↔ a second, non-synaptic pathway for ANNs

 
 The leap
 
 ANNs have exactly one communication mechanism: weighted connections — the brain's
 “synapses.” Give a network the brain's other channel: a low-dimensional
 continuous field over a 2D embedding of the units, governed by a learned damped-wave
 PDE. Units write into the field (a leaky projection of their activations) and read from
 it (an additive pre-activation bias) every step — slow, diffuse, spatial, bidirectional.
 An “astrocyte variant” adds dedicated non-spiking units that only shape the field, mirroring
 the paper's argument from the 1 : 1.4 human astrocyte ratio.
 
 Why it's open
 
 A literature check confirms zero ML implementations of bidirectional activation–field
 coupling; ephaptic modeling lives entirely in computational neuroscience. The “neural
 fields” name collision (§02-D) actively hides the direction from search.
 
 First experiment
 
 Bolt the field onto a small ViT or RNN; train on object-binding and rapid task-switching
 suites. Ablate each coupling direction. Decode task context from the field alone — if the
 stencil story is right, it should be linearly readable there.
 
 Success looks like
 
 Binding/switching gains at near-zero parameter cost, plus an interpretable, steerable
 control surface — you can perturb the field at inference (“tACS for transformers”) and
 watch behavior shift predictably.
 
 Risk
 
 Collapses into a fancy normalization layer. The kill criterion: if spatial structure in
 the field carries no decodable task information, the mechanism isn't doing what the
 theory says.
 
 
 

 
 P2Wave-Field State Spaces

 traveling waves as memory (C5) ↔ LinOSS / SSMs

 
 The leap
 
 LinOSS won an ICLR oral with harmonic oscillators as the recurrence — but its state
 matrix is diagonal: every oscillator is deliberately isolated, because uncoupled dynamics
 is what makes the parallel scan fast. No coupling, no traveling waves, none of the
 wave-borne memory Keller's wave-RNNs demonstrated. Couple the oscillators into a lattice
 and the latent state becomes a wave field.
 
 Why it's open
 
 The field walked away from coupling for speed reasons, not scientific ones. And there's
 a free lunch nobody has picked up: make the coupling circulant
 (translation-invariant on a ring/torus) and the FFT diagonalizes it — traveling waves
 become the eigenmodes, and the parallel scan survives in spectral space. Wave memory at
 SSM cost.
 
 First experiment
 
 Circulant-coupled LinOSS vs. LinOSS, Mamba, and wave-RNNs on Long Range Arena plus
 streaming recall; visualize the latent for emergent wavefronts (recency should be
 encoded as distance from the front).
 
 Success looks like
 Matched or better long-sequence accuracy with fewer parameters and an inspectable, physically meaningful memory.
 Risk
 Coupling could hurt optimization stability; D-LinOSS's learned damping is the natural regularizer to port.
 
 

 
 P3Interference Attention

 wave summation as computation (C6) ↔ holographic memory · grokking's Fourier circuits

 
 The leap
 
 Attention retrieves by comparing every query against every key — O(n²) explicit
 bookkeeping. Interference retrieves by superposition: encode key–value pairs as phasors
 summed into one hologram (vector-symbolic binding), retrieve by constructive
 interference. The KV cache becomes a fixed-size wave state.
 
 Why it's open
 
 The pieces have never been assembled: vector-symbolic architectures proved phasor
 binding works decades ago; grokking interpretability proved gradient descent
 spontaneously invents Fourier-interference algorithms when a task demands it;
 Selective Synchronization Attention (Feb 2026) just replaced dot-products with Kuramoto
 phase-locking — citing the physics, not the neuroscience, and not the VSA math either.
 Three communities, one mechanism, no synthesis.
 
 First experiment
 
 Hybrid heads (half dot-product, half phasor-holographic) on associative-recall
 synthetics and induction-head tasks, then perplexity at matched FLOPs; chart where
 superposition crosstalk (the known VSA capacity limit) bites vs. context length.
 
 Success looks like
 Comparable recall with O(1) memory per head and better length extrapolation — positional information living in phase, as the SSA result already hints.
 Risk
 Crosstalk scales badly with items stored; the contribution is mapping the regime where interference wins, not claiming it always does.
 
 

 
 P4Spectral Stencils

 spatial computing · Fig. 7 wave algebra (C3/C6) ↔ task arithmetic · FiLM · continual learning

 
 The leap
 
 The paper's control theory, translated literally: a slow controller network emits
 coefficients over a band-limited spatial wave basis; synthesis produces a smooth gating
 field over the fast network's unit lattice; contexts compose by adding
 coefficients — the paper's Fig. 7, where two context-waves sum to four task
 programs. ML already has the receipts that additive composition works: task arithmetic
 and steering vectors compose by vector addition, unexplained. This gives that empirical
 magic its missing theory — those vectors are flat stencils — and its missing structure:
 make them spatial, band-limited, and cheap.
 
 Why it's open
 
 Gating exists everywhere (FiLM, adapters, MoE); spatially structured, wave-composed,
 low-frequency gating exists nowhere — ML gating has no topology because ML units
 have no locations. Giving units locations is the unexamined prerequisite the brain gets
 for free.
 
 First experiment
 
 Continual-learning and compositional-generalization suites vs. FiLM/LoRA/context-gating
 baselines at matched parameters; then the closing-the-loop test — measure whether gated
 units reproduce PFC statistics: mixed-selectivity fractions, subspace
 orthogonalization, error-from-stencil-mismatch.
 
 Success looks like
 
 Catastrophic-forgetting resistance (context gating already showed this in 2018) plus
 zero-shot task composition by coefficient addition — and, if the loop closes, an ANN
 result that counts as evidence for the neuroscience.
 
 Risk
 Band-limiting may be the wrong inductive bias for non-spatial tasks; the ablation against unconstrained gating isolates exactly what the wave structure buys.
 
 

 
 P5The Thalamic Clock

 theta master timing · communication through coherence (C2) ↔ mixture-of-experts routing

 
 The leap
 
 Every MoE router in the 2026 literature is a feedforward classifier scoring tokens.
 The brain routes differently: a subcortical pacemaker sets phase relationships, and
 regions exchange information only when their excitability windows align —
 communication through coherence. Translation: give experts oscillatory phases set by a
 small Kuramoto coordinator; inter-expert messages pass in proportion to phase alignment;
 routing becomes a dynamical schedule rather than a per-token decision, and
 frequency-division lets multiple token streams share the same experts without
 interference.
 
 Why it's open
 A targeted search of 2025–26 MoE routing work finds hierarchical, eigenbasis, self-routing variants — and no dynamical or phase-based coordination at all.
 First experiment
 Small MoE with phase-gated expert messaging on multi-task suites with known interference structure; measure cross-task contamination vs. learned-router baselines, plus AKOrN-style robustness side-effects.
 Success looks like
 Lower cross-task interference and emergent expert scheduling — specialization in time, not just in parameters.
 Risk
 Coupled-oscillator training instability; AKOrN's discretization tricks and SSA's closed-form steady state are the two available stabilizers.
 
 

 Learning & dynamics

 
 P6Wave-Scheduled Consolidation

 rotating sleep waves gate plasticity (C5) ↔ replay buffers · continual learning

 
 The leap
 
 Muller 2016: rotating waves during sleep spindles sweep cortex, opening
 spike-timing-dependent-plasticity windows in an organized spatial sequence — consolidation
 is scheduled by a wave. ML replay is i.i.d.: sample uniformly, update everywhere.
 Translation: phase-gate learning rates — a slow wave over the unit/parameter lattice
 determines which region may consolidate now — and order replay along the wave's path.
 A second, data-free variant mirrors retinal waves: pretrain on self-generated traveling-wave
 activity as a structural curriculum before any data arrives.
 
 Why it's open
 Cyclical learning-rate schedules vary plasticity in time only; nothing varies it over network space, and wave-pretraining exists only as a couple of vision-neuroscience studies.
 First experiment
 Continual-learning benchmarks: phase-gated learning rates + wave-ordered replay vs. EWC and uniform replay; ablate the spatial component against pure schedules.
 Success looks like
 Less interference at equal replay budget — consolidation that doesn't overwrite what a concurrent region is protecting.
 Risk
 May reduce to a rediscovery of cyclical schedules; the spatial term must demonstrably earn its keep or the proposal dies.
 
 

 
 P7Latent Itinerancy

 distraction-and-return trajectories (C5) ↔ latent chain-of-thought

 
 The leap
 
 Batabyal 2026 (cited in the paper): distraction knocks PFC population activity off its
 trajectory, and wave dynamics shepherd it back. Latent reasoning research (COCONUT and
 successors) currently treats continuous thought as a sequence of opaque vectors with no
 dynamical theory of stability. Model reasoning steps as metastable states in a wave
 field, transitions driven by a control field — thought as itinerancy between attractors
 rather than token emission.
 
 Why it's open
 The latent-reasoning community optimizes benchmarks, not dynamics; nobody has asked what makes a latent thought robust, and the neuroscience just handed over both the concept and the experimental design.
 First experiment
 Diagnostic before architecture: perturb latent reasoning states mid-chain with calibrated noise, measure return-to-trajectory vs. derailment as a function of where in the chain the hit lands — the direct ML mirror of the monkey distraction experiment.
 Success looks like
 A stability map of latent reasoning (which steps are attractor-protected, which are knife-edges), then architectures that widen the basins.
 Risk
 Metastability may not be how flat transformers do it at all — a null result here is itself a finding about how un-brain-like latent reasoning is.
 
 

 Evaluation & interpretability

 
 P8Anesthesiology for AI

 anesthetic convergence · PCI (C7) ↔ agent-coherence evaluation

 
 The leap
 
 Consciousness science solved a problem AI evaluation still has: how to measure whether a
 system is globally integrated without asking it. Zap cortex with TMS, compress
 the echo — the perturbational complexity index cleanly separates awake from anesthetized
 brains. Port it: perturb a model's activations mid-inference with standardized pulses,
 record the propagation pattern across layers and timesteps, compress it. High complexity
 = the perturbation was integrated and differentiated; low = it either died
 locally (fragmentation) or exploded uniformly (stereotypy).
 
 Why it's open
 Verified against the 2026 literature: PCI has been applied to brain simulations, never to LLMs or agents. It is architecture-agnostic, training-free, and cheap.
 First experiment
 
 Compute model-PCI across checkpoints, quantization levels, and context lengths; test the
 signature hypothesis — that PCI drops before task metrics do as a model is
 degraded (pruning, quantization, context overflow), making it an early-warning gauge for
 long-horizon agent incoherence. Adopt the paper's own methodology: many mechanistically
 different perturbations converging on one dynamical signature means the signature is
 load-bearing.
 
 Success looks like
 A leading indicator for agent breakdown that needs no benchmark and no ground truth.
 Risk
 Complexity measures are notoriously confoundable by scale and architecture; matched-checkpoint comparisons are the discipline that keeps it honest.
 
 

 
 P9Wave Interpretability

 stencil mismatch predicts errors (C3) ↔ mechanistic interpretability

 
 The leap
 
 Mechanistic interpretability is overwhelmingly static — features, circuits, weights. The
 first dynamical studies (Fernando & Guitchounts 2025: residual-stream units trace
 periodic orbits; attractor-like lower layers) found oscillation without a framework for
 what it's for. This paper supplies the framework: look for a slow/fast
 decomposition of residual dynamics — a low-frequency control component gating a
 high-frequency content component — the spectral signature of stencils. Then run Chen
 2026's monkey logic on models: their stencil–context mismatch predicted behavioral errors
 before the response; the analog is predicting hallucinations from control-subspace
 geometry before generation.
 
 Why it's open
 The dynamical-interpretability thread is months old, tiny, and has not met the control-theoretic reading; hallucination detection today reads outputs and probes, not control dynamics.
 First experiment
 Koopman/DMD-style mode decomposition over layer-time activation trajectories; correlate slow-mode misalignment (context vs. content) with factuality failures on standard hallucination sets.
 Success looks like
 An online, output-independent hallucination early-warning signal — and independent evidence that trained transformers contain a control plane worth naming.
 Risk
 Depth-as-time is not time; the decomposition may not transfer across prompts. Start with recurrent-ish settings (long generation) where real temporal dynamics exist.
 
 

 Hardware

 
 P10Split-Substrate Hardware

 digital storage + analog coordination (C6) ↔ photonic / analog accelerators

 
 The leap
 
 The entire analog-hardware industry targets the matmul — the layer the brain implements
 with discrete spikes and synapses. The brain spends its analog physics on the
 other layer: control, routing, gating. The paper's architecture, taken
 seriously as a hardware spec, is a split substrate — digital cores for storage and
 content, with an analog interference control bus: a photonic or RF layer that
 synthesizes gating fields (the spectral stencils of P4) by superposition at light speed
 and near-zero marginal energy, broadcast to the digital cores without per-step
 conversion. A second, self-contained piece: an optical front-end that computes the
 grassfire/medial-axis transform by literal wave interference — shape-skeleton features
 that directly attack vision models' documented texture bias.
 
 Why it's open
 Photonics roadmaps (100+ TOPS/W demonstrations) are all matmul accelerators; neuromorphics is all spikes. Nobody builds analog control over digital content — the one split the biology actually endorses. The parametron — 1950s phase-logic computing — is the forgotten ancestor waiting to be cited.
 First experiment
 No fab needed: simulate the split — P4's spectral stencils with control-path costs set to photonic energy budgets — and publish the systems argument; the medial-axis front-end can be prototyped in simulation against texture-bias benchmarks immediately.
 Success looks like
 An architecture paper that redirects some fraction of analog-hardware effort from the matmul war to the uncontested control plane.
 Risk
 Analog dreams die at the converter. The proposal lives or dies on keeping the control field analog end-to-end — broadcast gating natively, never digitizing per step.
 
 

 
 05 · Honest limits
 Where the mapping breaks

 

 
 
 The epiphenomenon risk transfers asymmetrically. If waves turn out to be
 cortical exhaust, the neuroscience theory collapses — but the ML proposals don't: an
 inductive bias justified by benchmarks needs no biological license. This asymmetry makes
 §04 cheap insurance: each proposal is simultaneously an engineering bet and a test of the
 theory. If spectral stencils (P4) fail to produce mixed selectivity, that's evidence
 against spatial computing, publishable either way.
 

 
 Translate problems, not mechanisms. Some brain solutions are workarounds
 for constraints ANNs don't have — millisecond synapses, wiring cost, no weight sharing, no
 global learning signal. The filter used throughout §04: import the problem waves
 solve (routing without a homunculus, binding, scheduling, coordination) and let the
 mechanism re-derive itself in silico. Copying wave physics into a GPU for its own sake
 imports the costume, not the actor.
 

 
 The energy argument does not survive simulation. Waves computed in
 float32 cost FLOPs like everything else. The 20-watt story cashes out only in physical
 substrates (P10); software claims (P1–P7) must win on capability, robustness, or
 interpretability instead — and are framed to do so.
 

 
 Consciousness is not an engineering target here. What transfers is the
 functional skeleton — global integration as a measurable regime (P8). Nothing in this
 document bears on phenomenal experience, and the paper's own consciousness chapter is its
 most speculative (C8).
 

 
 Evidence concentration is real. The stencil results rest chiefly on
 Miller-lab primate data with one strong independent support (Fang 2025); ephaptic
 causality at cognitive scale is inferred, not shown. Anyone building on C3–C6 should track
 the replication record — and P4/P9's closing-the-loop tests are designed to feed it.
 

 
 The two-hues caveat. This review's sharpest claim — ML imported waves as
 content but not control — is an argument from absence, verified by search but always
 vulnerable to an unindexed preprint. It is stated as of August 2026.
